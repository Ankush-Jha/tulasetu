Proposed Solution (Describe your Idea / Solution / Prototype)

Detailed explanation of the proposed solution

  • A deterministic calculation engine — every cell of the report is computed, never typed.
  • 17 R 76-2 tests reduce to 6 kernels + one multi-interval-aware mpe resolver: 36 form pages, 34 rules, 363 §17 items.
  • Officer's path: profile → test list → observations → checklist → attachments → PDF + DOCX + JSON.

How it addresses the problem

The application half of model approval is already digital on eMaap. From "model received at the RRSL" onward it is a spreadsheet — which the PS itself calls time-consuming, error-prone and lacking uniformity. We digitise that half.

  • Errors: the band is resolved per load and per partial range — the trap a spreadsheet falls into.
  • Uniformity: "– –" carries the clause that excuses it, so no two RRSLs can disagree.

Innovation and uniqueness of the solution

  • Family / EUT solver (§3.10.4) — the minimum variant set to physically test, writing the certificate's own "shall also cover…" clause.
  • Annex F module compatibility — indicator + load cell checked on n, Emax, vmin, temperature overlap, Σpᵢ² ≤ 1.
  • Dual conformity profile — Seventh Schedule or OIML (Rule 7(1)(a)), with IV ↔ IIII normalisation since India's own rules use both.
  • Bilingual, offline, role-aware — Hindi + English on the printed report; runs on one lab PC, no internet.
  • No AI in the legal path, by design — OIML holds AI is not yet court-proof for legal metrology.

Impact and Benefits

Smart India Hackathon2026

Potential impact on the target audience

  • RRSL officers — the arithmetic and the re-checking disappear; the officer's judgement stays. Report preparation moves from days of spreadsheet work to the length of the bench test.
  • Assistant / Deputy Directors and the Director (LM) — review a computed report with an audit trail, and the dashboard files the annual return that Rule 4(9) already demands: models tested, fees realised, time taken.
  • Indian manufacturers and MSMEs — since 2023 India may issue OIML certificates, letting them export without paying for testing abroad. A correct R 76 report is what that certificate rests on; a rejection instead costs a fresh application and fresh fees under Rule 10.
  • Consumers — every shop scale, jeweller's balance, ration-shop weigher and weighbridge reaches the market through this gate. A faster, uniform gate is a more trustworthy one.

Benefits of the solution (social, economic, environmental, etc.)

  • Economic — the second halving. After model-approval services went online the end-to-end timeline fell from 6–9 months to 3–4 months (DoCA Annual Report 2022-23). Digitising the application produced that first halving; the report chain is what remains inside the 3–4 months.
  • Social — uniformity is fairness. One engine and one versioned pack mean a manufacturer meets the same yardstick at every RRSL, and a citizen the same protection in every State.
  • Institutional — India's first structured NAWI test dataset. Reports become machine-readable JSON instead of filing-cabinet PDFs, opening failure-pattern analysis by model family.
  • Regulatory fit. Jan Vishwas moves legal metrology toward trust-based, facilitative enforcement; an append-only, signed record is exactly what a lighter-touch regime needs to stay auditable.
  • Environmental. A 62-page report per model, printed and circulated for signature, becomes one signed electronic record.

  • OIML D 31:2019 software for measuring instruments · B 18:2022 OIML-CS · ISO/IEC 17025:2017 §7.8

Indian legal framework

  • Legal Metrology Act, 2009 — s.22 model approval, s.24 verification; in force 1 April 2011
  • LM (General) Rules, 2011 — Rule 13 → Seventh Schedule, Heading A; Rule 27 re-verification periods. Covers about 40 kinds of instrument, of which NAWIs are one.
  • LM (Approval of Models) Rules, 2011 (+2019 amendment) — R.4(3) laboratory conditions, R.4(9) annual return, R.5 application contents, R.7(1)(a) Indian rules or OIML, R.8 report flow, R.10 resubmission fees, R.11(4) publication, R.19 fees, R.20 State licence gate
  • LM (Government Approved Test Centre) Rules, 2013 — scope widened in October 2025 to 18 categories of instrument
  • Jan Vishwas Acts, 2023 and 2026 — decriminalisation and improvement notices; Information Technology Act, 2000 §§4 and 5

How the research became code

  • Both standards were extracted to searchable text with page markers, so every threshold in a rule pack cites the page it came from and can be re-checked by grep.
  • Golden tests were written from the standards' worked examples before the kernels — those examples are the acceptance criteria, not fixtures of our own.
  • Heading A was compared line by line with OIML: Table 17, Table 20, the in-service ×2 factor and the multi-interval table are identical at the primary-source level.

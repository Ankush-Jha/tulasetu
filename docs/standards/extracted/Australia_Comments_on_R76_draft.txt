AU    ge It is not clear whether the scope of the current 
review is intended to consider technical 
changes. Or whether it is limited to formatting 
and structural changes. Australia has concerns 
with some of the technical changes being 
proposed. Australia does have technical issues 
that it wants considered and we have included 
some technical comments as part of this 
revision, however we suggest technical 
changes be deferred to a more substantive 
review. 
  
AU 1 3.1.2 Note 1 ed A non-automatic weighing process allows the 
operator to take an action (i.e., adjust the unit 
price) 
Adjusting the unit price won’t influence the 
weighing result, only the transaction result.   
Delete ‘adjust the unit price’ or reword by changing 
‘influences the weighing result’ to the transaction or 
measurement result. 
 
AU 1 5.5.1 1 te As 5.5.3.3 regarding exception for preset tare has 
been removed.  Now MPEs also apply to 
calculated net value when a preset tare device is in 
use.  Net value is not calculated net value and tare 
device is not the same as preset tare device. The 
statement ‘The MPEs apply to the net value when a 
tare is in use’ is not 100% correct. 
The MPEs apply to the net value/calculated net value when a 
tare/preset tare is in use. 
 
R76-2 5.6.1 is the test for tare device.  Should the similar test 
to R76-2 5.6.1 to be implemented? 
 
 
 
AU 1 5.5.2  ge 
Australia strongly objects to the proposed content of 
5.5.2 and the doubling of MPEs for in-service 
inspections. Australia’s legislated in-service MPE’s 
are equal to the value of Verification MPEs. 
There is no definition of an in-service inspection. 
Remove all reference to in-service inspection and in-service 
MPEs. 
 
We suggest that the topic of in-service MPEs be removed from 
the scope of this revision of OIML R 76 The topic of in-
service inspections and MPEs has significant implications at a 
national level and should be deferred to the next full revision 
of OIML R 76 to allow for the necessary consultation and 
analysis processes to occur to support the definition and 
introduction of in-service MPEs 
 
AU 1 7.1.10  te Clause 7.1.3.2, Semi-automatic tare devices, does 
not permit reduction of tare value, but permits the 
increase of tare value.  In the self-service bring 
own container case (an increased trend), it is 
possible for a customer to tare off the empty 
container first then fill the container with product 
and tare off the partially filled container, then fill 
the container again with more product.  The 
customer only pays for the last part of fill.   
Add: The action of the tare device must not permit an 
increase of a value of the tare when using the same 
container.

AU 2 5.10  ge For Verification testing, the current 
Australian requirements are 2/3 (66.66%) 
Max. On a Class III 15 kg capacity instrument 
as an example this would equate to 10 kg – 
the OIML standard is 14 kg. Likewise with a 
pallet scale of 1,500 kg capacity would 
require 1,000 kg at 2/3 Max – or 1,200 kg at 
0.8 Max. A 1,000 kg test using one block is 
simpler, more efficient and safer than e.g. a 
1,000 kg block with 10 x 20 kg blocks sitting 
on top. 
At 2/3 capacity, typically the 2nd changeover 
point (2000e for a 3000 VSI Instrument), 
therefore determination of any error is made 
easier also. 
 
5.10 Repeatability test (R 76-1, 5.5.4) 
For type approval two series of weighings shall be 
performed, one with a load of about 50 % and one with a 
load close to 100 % of Max. For instruments with Max 
less than 1 000 kg each series shall consist of ten 
weighings. In other cases each series shall consist of at 
least three weighings. Readings shall be taken when the 
instrument is loaded, and when the unloaded instrument 
has come to rest between weighings. In the case of a 
zero deviation between the weighings, the instrument 
shall be reset to zero, without determining the error at 
zero. The true zero position need not be determined 
between the weighings. 
If the instrument is provided with automatic zero-setting 
or zero-tracking, it shall be in operation during the test. 
For verification one series of weighings with 2/3 Max is 
sufficient. Three weighings on classes III and IV or six 
weighings on classes I and II are necessary.

AU 5 6 Table 5 ge Australia does not legislate a reverification 
period for NAWI’s excepting public 
weighbridges. Noting this we provide the 
following as part of our submission. 
We strongly disagree with the varying 
verification periods being based on “intensive 
use”, especially when 10 weighing’s per week 
on average is the decisive number. We point 
out that recording the number of weighings a 
NAWI is used for would be impractical to 
regulate. 
Consideration could be given to some sort of 
“Class Based” system as being a fairer and 
more appropriate option. Generally, Class I, II 
and smaller capacity Class III instruments are 
more cared for and in better environments, 
whereas Class IV and Y (Front End Loaders, 
etc) are used extensively with many factors 
that can affect weighing performance. Such 
factors in weighbridges include, but are not 
limited to, mud build up around the load cells, 
build up under the decks and general wear 
and tear of the load cells and retaining plates. 
Similar issues can also apply to Pallet scales 
of up to 3 tonne capacity. Class Y instruments 
could have hydraulic parts replaced which can 
affect weighing performance.  Mechanical 
Instruments are more inclined to wear over 
time and have greater potential of binding and 
fouling in their mechanisms. 
In short, Class I, II, and smaller capacity 
Class III instruments operate off a single 
point load cell, everything else has moving 
parts which can wear or get inhibited in their 
performance. 
Option 1: 
Class I 2 years 
Class II 2 years 
Class III Single Point load cell system 2 years 
All other Class III 1 year 
Class IV & Y and mechanical 1 year 
 
Option 2: 
Class I 2 years 
Class II 2 years 
Class III <= 300 kg 2 years 
Class III > 300 kg) 1 year 
Class IV & Y and mechanical 1 year 
 
 
AU 5 6 Table 5 ge A recommendation should also be inserted 
that a check / inspection be conducted where 
weighing instruments have been relocated 
from the verification location, to ensure that 
Gravity change or manual handling of this 
relocation has not altered the weighing 
instruments metrological performance.  
Add note: Where environment conditions impact the 
ability for the weighing instrument to maintain usability 
and display accuracy then a re-verification of less than 
the recommended verification interval may be required. 
In such case a Risk assessment may need to be 
conducted to determine suitable re-verification period
